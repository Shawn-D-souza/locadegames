# Territory

Format: **arena** (one real-time match for the whole room). Catalog id `territory`, 2–8 players.

An original territory-capture game in the style of Paper.io 2 (our own look and rules, not a copy). Leave your land, draw a trail, come home: the trail and everything it closed off become your land.

> This doc is Territory's source of truth: rules, owner decisions, how it works, its look and its files. Platform-wide rules, the arena format and the room flow are in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md); the shared visual language is in [`DESIGN.md`](../../DESIGN.md). Update this doc in the same change whenever something about Territory changes.

---

## Rules (owner decisions, 2026-10-31)

- **Match:** everyone in the room plays one match together. 3 s countdown; at most 3 minutes; ends early when one player (or none) is left.
- **Arena:** round. Its radius grows with √players (`arenaBaseRadius + arenaRadiusPerSqrtPlayer · √n`). Everyone starts on a small disc of land on a ring around the middle, heading along the ring.
- **Steering:** 360°. Drag anywhere on the screen: an invisible floating joystick (nothing drawn, owner decision 2026-11-01); the head turns toward the drag direction at a fast but limited rate ("quick arcs"). Arrows / WASD on desktop.
- **Capturing:** outside your land you leave a trail. Get back home and the trail (its full width) plus every area it closed off becomes your land, taken from anyone who had it. Areas closed off against the wall count too, except the biggest one (that is the rest of the arena). **You're home as your head touches your land (owner decision, 2026-11-08):** in it, or with the head's center within `homeReach` (0.3 trail widths) of it; before, the center had to be in, so half or more of the drawn head was already in the land, which felt too deep. Leaving home takes the same distance, so sliding along your own edge never captures over and over. A trail still starts and ends exactly on the land's edge (the edge point nearest the head when it is just outside). The same code (`home.ts`) runs on the server and in your phone's prediction.
- **Exact land (hard requirement):** captured land is exactly the shape drawn: sharp turns stay sharp, curves stay curves, no grid and no smoothing after capture. The trail starts exactly at the edge of your land; neighbours' land meets with no gaps or seams; captures along the wall reach the wall.
- **Out:** someone's head touching your trail cuts you (crossing your own trail is allowed). Losing all your land, or quitting, also puts you out. **No respawns:** you watch the rest.
- **Land cut in two (owner decision, 2026-11-01):** when someone's capture cuts a piece of your land into parts, you keep only the part you're standing on; the other parts **vanish** (nobody gets them, so the leader can't snowball by slicing small players). Out on a trail, you keep the part your trail left from (or the nearest part to it). Chosen for fun: a leader can't safely sit inside their land until time runs out, comebacks stay possible, and slicing is a gamble because the slicer's trail runs through land where the owner moves freely. **No exceptions (owner decision, 2026-11-07):** your land is always one piece (land won by a cut is joined to yours, see below), so this applies to all of it.
- **Trapping:** a loop that takes **all** of someone's land knocks them out, credited to the player who took it ("Ana took all your land."). Trapping only their head (they're out on a trail) is not a kill. **Slivers count as nothing (2026-11-01):** a leftover strip thinner than `minLandThickness` (0.4 trail widths) vanishes, so taking someone's land but for an invisible edge knocks them out.
- **Full lap (owner decision, 2026-11-01):** going all the way round the arena along the wall and home captures everything inside, which is everyone's land: an instant win (~44 s alone with 2 players, ~65 s with 8). Kept as a legendary high-risk play: everyone is inside the loop, so anyone can cut it. (Trails are no longer on the minimap, owner decision 2026-11-01, so a lap is harder to see coming; watch for this in playtests.) If playtests show laps win too often, the fallback is: an area with a living opponent's head inside is not captured.
- **Land of a player who goes out:** goes to the player who cut them (as in Paper.io). **Joined by the trails (owner decision, 2026-11-07):** the cut player's trail from their land to where it was cut, and the cutter's own trail back to the cutter's land, become the cutter's land too, so the won land is joined to theirs in one piece (the cut trail past the cut does not). **Any open area those trails close off is filled, like a capture** (owner decision, 2026-11-08), and the open rest of the arena never is. Land under those trails is taken from anyone else, like a capture (their land cut in two keeps one part; left with none, they're out). If they went out any other way, it vanishes. A quitter's land vanishes (my call; owner may change).
- **Heads are solid:** they never overlap. Meeting head-on, both stop and nobody is out; steering away frees them; a glancing touch slides past. The collision disc is as wide as the drawn head, so heads stop when they touch, like at the wall.
- **Wall:** heads slide along it.
- **Away players** (locked phone, lost connection) keep going straight (sliding along the wall). No timeout.
- **Cut timing:** favour the victim. The server judges at its own moment, with no rewind (no lag compensation). Within one step, captures are processed before cuts.
- **Ranking:** players still in, by land share; then players who are out, by how long they lasted. Ties share a place; everyone in first place is a champion (shared win) and gets a room trophy.
- **Server restart mid-match:** the match is lost and the room goes back to its hub (my call).

Split detail (`keepConnected` in `capture.ts`): you keep the part nearest to where you stand (0 when you're on it): your head, or your trail's start while you're out. A part is a sliver when shrinking it by half of `minLandThickness` leaves nothing; slivers are dropped first.

Join detail (`stepTerritory`, `joinLand`): the cut trail counts up to its point closest to the cutter's head, then runs on to the cutter's head (a head cuts from up to `cutReach` away), so the strip reaches them. The trails are stroked to the full trail width, like a capture's, and newly enclosed open areas are filled the same way (`fillClosedOff`, shared with `captureLand`). In a chain of cuts in one step, a cutter who is also out passes on their whole trail, so everything reaches the first player still in. The cutter's trail ends there (phones end a trail when the server says that head is home).

Cut detail: you are cut as soon as another head visibly touches your trail: its center within its radius plus half a trail width of the trail's center line (`cutReach`), except your trail's last `headGraceLength` (right behind your own head). When several heads cross a trail in one step, the closest cuts it (then the lowest id). If the cutter is also out that step, the land goes to the first player still in along the chain of cutters.

## Numbers (`src/shared/games/territory/config.ts`)

Integer world units; the trail is 1000 wide. 20 steps/s. Speed 320 per step (6.4 trail widths/s). Headings in 4096 steps; turning at most 280 per step (~490°/s). Head collision radius 600. Home within 300 of your land. Arena radius 20,000 + 15,000·√players (360-gon). Start land: radius 3000 (48-gon) on a ring at 0.55 of the radius.

## How it works

- **Land model:** exact polygons in integer world units, with [Clipper2](https://github.com/AngusJohnson/Clipper2) (`clipper2-ts`). A capture is: union(land, the trail stroked to its full width with round joins and ends, plus a "skirt" beyond the wall wherever the trail runs along it) ∩ arena, then every newly enclosed open area is filled. Open areas come from a polytree of arena − land; new ones are matched to old ones by exact area + bounds; wall-touching ones are found with a thin band inside the wall.
- **Movement:** deterministic integer math (precomputed step vectors per heading), clamped inside the wall, so the client's prediction lands on the same units as the server.
- **Server:** the region Durable Object runs the match in memory, stepping on a 25 ms `setInterval` (only while a match runs, so the DO hibernates otherwise). After a stall it skips time instead of fast-forwarding. Inputs carry the step they apply on; the server queues them (clamped to a lead limit) and counts late ones.
- **Wire (binary, `wire.ts`):** a snapshot per step (every head: position, heading, target, last applied input, late count; plus events: trail start, capture with the new land polygons of everyone affected, out). A full sync on join, reconnect or a client's `sync` request. Pong carries client time, server time and the time of step 0. Client → server: input (seq, step, target, only when the steering changes, at most one per step, quantized), ping, sync. Points are zigzag-varint deltas.
- **Client netcode (`src/web/games/territory/engine/`):**
  - Server clock from the fastest round trips among recent pings, slewed gently.
  - Others drawn ~100 ms+ in the past, interpolated; the delay adapts to how late snapshots arrive. **It never moves anyone backwards:** it grows gently (others slow to at least 70% speed for a moment) and shrinks slowly. After a hiccup others are guessed onward in a straight line for up to 3 steps, then hold; when the late steps arrive they carry on from where they were drawn (the wait becomes extra delay), and the gap between the guess and the real path glides out (60 ms half-life) instead of snapping.
  - **Your own head is predicted ahead** by rtt/2 + 2·jitter + 20 ms (+ a boost while the server reports late inputs). That lead is re-measured on every ping but eased into (at most 6% faster or slower meanwhile; a change over 250 ms snaps), since moving it at once moves your head along its path in one frame. Inputs are stamped with the predicted step, so the server applies them on the same step. The prediction is recomputed every frame from the server's latest state plus unacknowledged inputs; **any change to where your head was drawn a moment ago** (your own steering reaching the step it's heading for, a late input, the server's answer) is eased out (60 ms half-life), so steering never makes it hop sideways.
  - Other heads block yours in the prediction with the server's own code (they keep steering toward their last known target, as the server does). On screen your head is also kept out of others' drawn heads, since they are drawn in the past.
  - Your own captures are predicted with the server's capture code and swapped for the server's when it arrives (they are identical; a test checks it). Your land is drawn in two layers so land you just lost stays until the other player's capture is drawn.
  - Your own events, and knockouts you caused, show at once; the rest when their step is drawn.
  - A gap in steps or a woken tab asks for a full sync.
- **Measured:** full flow at 60 fps in headless Chromium; heads never closer than touching in a live head-on test. Smoothness (2 players circling, every drawn frame's head speed against the true speed, through a proxy adding delay, jitter and spikes): no network: worst frame 11% off for your head (was 142% before the lead and steering glides), others steady; 40–70 ms one-way + a 300 ms spike in 40 chunks: your head's worst 56%, others never jump (was 830%), no resyncs.

## Cost (free tier)

The DO stays awake while a match runs, and every input message is a DO request. Measured: an 8-player, 3-minute match uses about 3 s of CPU in total (0.8 ms per step on average, worst 42 ms); snapshots average ~335 B (~6.7 KB/s per player). Watch the DO request and GB-s numbers once people play it.

## Look

Full screen, drawn on a canvas (Canvas 2D), every color from the theme. No header; the HUD floats on top of the arena.

- **Arena:** a round floor in the platform's pale `background` color with the dot grid and an ink wall drawn over everything, **the same thin line all around** (owner decision, 2026-11-08: a lip and a flat shadow under the bottom edge, to look like a raised table, made the bottom border look thick and were dropped); beyond the wall is a deeper calm `inkSoft`. The minimap's floor stays white. The camera follows your own head (about 18 trail widths across the short side); watchers, and you once you're out, see the whole arena.
- **Land:** each player's land is a flat shape in their color, **raised** on a solid lip in its dark shade (like the buttons), exactly the shape that was drawn. A light band runs along its top edges, as if lit from above. **Pattern (owner decision, 2026-11-07): studs**, round pegs raised on the land like a game board, the same for every player in their own color, fixed to the world. Drawn with depth in flat colors (the lip trick used inside the land: a darker side under each stud, a light top edge, light from above), on one cached tile per player. Other patterns are ready in `landPatterns.ts` (holes, tiles, grooves, stars, rivets; `look.landPattern.kind`) for when players can pick their own; rejected: flat stickers (a different small shape per color), flat stripes / dots / waves (too plain). Neighbours' land meets with no seam (a hairline same-color stroke hides anti-aliasing seams). Others' new land flashes white and fades (450 ms). **Your own new land fills in like Paper.io's** (owner decision, 2026-11-08, from a Paper.io recording; first tried a pop (the land swelling and settling) and small white sparkles sitting on it, which "do not match the game"; then glitter specks on the filling land and streaks flying from it into the head with a glint (Paper.io's sparkles that travel to the player), removed too; a floating "+%" was turned down as annoying): it shows at once in a pale shade of your color, then your color spreads across it from where your head got home (a little over a quarter of a second, slowing as it goes; owner: "slightly faster" than the first third of a second), with a light shine just behind the front. About 0.6 s in all, on your phone only (`look.captureFill`, `drawFills`). **Every head moving through its own land kicks up sand behind it** (owner decision, 2026-11-08, spotted in Paper.io as a very light motion behind the cube; theirs looks like surfing through water, ours first looked like smoke, then "slightly" more like sand): every 90 ms while it moves (nothing standing still or out of its land), a faint puff in a lighter shade of the land a little behind it, swaying side to side, growing, drifting aside and fading in 0.4 s, and two small grains flicked out to the sides and a little back in a tiny hop, one light, one a shade darker (left out of every third puff: owner, a little fewer dark grains), gone in 0.3 s (`look.wake`, `headWake.ts`); a handful of circles per head, on the phone only. Land cut off from its owner sinks and fades away (700 ms).
- **Trenches (owner idea, 2026-11-01):** a trail crossing someone's raised land runs at floor level in a trench cut through it: the land's dark side shows along the trench's far edge. Drawn by putting trails under the land and cutting each trail out of the land's lips and tops on a scratch layer (only when a trail actually crosses someone's land). **The trail keeps its full width in the trench whichever way it runs** (owner, 2026-11-08: going round in a circle on someone's land, parts of the trail looked thinner; the same day a trail lying on top of the land, with or without a faint shadow, was weighed and turned down: the trench gives the feel of being below the raised land): the lips are cut exactly where the trail is, and the tops there and also one lip higher, so the far wall shows beyond the trail instead of covering its edge (before, the lips' cut was shifted down by the lip, and the wall covered up to a third of the trail wherever it ran sideways). The cut is 1.5 device px narrower on each side than the trail, so the land overlaps the trail's edge and no white anti-aliasing line shows between them.
- **Trail:** a clean band in a lighter tint of the player's color, edged in the full color, starting exactly at the edge of their land, with round joins.
- **Head (2026-11-07):** a soft cube faked in 3D, turning with its heading (owner: "Paper.io's characters feel 3D"; real 3D with Three.js was weighed and left for later as too big a step). Seen from above at an angle: the top is squashed top to bottom; every side facing us is its own face, shaded by which way it points (light from the upper left), so as the cube turns its sides turn through the light like a real object; a flat shadow on the ground. **The cube stands on its spot** (owner: the trail and the character weren't lined up): its bottom sits where the head is, so the trail runs in under it, and its top is raised one side's height; name tags and the winner's crown sit above the raised top. **Front and back** read from any angle by a **crest** (owner: like a bird's head feathers): three plump feathers standing up from the back of the top, leaning back and fanning out, in the player's color with a lighter middle; it rises above the cube even when the eyes are hidden. One soft outline in a dark shade of the player's own color around the whole cube and the feathers, none between top and sides (owner: a smooth cube, not two square outlines; ink outlines looked too heavy next to Paper.io). The **eyes are on the front face**, low, drawn onto the face so they turn with it (owner: eyes on top looked 2D): full when heading toward us, a sliver sideways, hidden from behind (the feather still shows the way). The pupils look into the turn (read from the drawn angles, eased, `headMotion.ts`; never from or into the simulation). **Blinks** at random times of its own (`headMotion.ts`, `look.headMotion`). **Hops only for joy (owner decision, 2026-11-08: the hop shows how happy it is):** each is a **fixed animation** (owner: predefined, not worked out from the land's size): a medium capture is a hop, a large one a bigger hop, a small one none (owner: a tiny hop looked bad); a knockout (cutting someone, or taking all their land) is a higher hop with a full spin (`look.headMotion.hops`). Medium and large are the same sizes the capture sounds use (`captureSize.ts`, `captureSize` in the client config: from 11 and 24 trail widths across, as areas). It lifts off its shadow, which shrinks; at the top wall it stays inside. The client adds these as `cheer` effects (yours at once, others' when their step is drawn). Tried and dropped the same day as too much motion: a hop all the time while moving (with squash on landing), leaning into turns, a squish on bumps. Tried and dropped: a flat feather lying on the top (too 2D), a plain turning cube (every side the same, no front or back), a block that always faces us with sliding eyes. The bottom's width is the head's solid disc, so heads touch where they're drawn. No garbage per frame (reused point arrays, the feather's paths made once).
- **HUD:** top left: ✕ (quit, asks "Quit this game?" first) and the clock in a white pill, which turns coral in the last 10 s. Top right: the land table, a small bar per player in their color (place, name, land share), best first, your own row with a lip and always shown. The clock, the places and the shares are in the game's number font, the same as the countdown and the result tag (the display font at its heaviest, owner decision 2026-11-08: bigger, chunkier numbers), the shares' decimals smaller (a big `7`, a small `.36%`). Under the clock: knockout notices ("Ana cut Ben", "You took Ben's land", "Ben quit"), at most 3, each for 3 s. Bottom left: the minimap (the arena and every land, redrawn 5 times a second; no trails, owner decision 2026-11-01), with every player drawn on it every frame as a tiny copy of their head (rounded block, ink outline and lip, turned the way they go; yours bigger and on top), so they glide instead of flickering.
- **Countdown:** a big numeral on a flat white disc (no lip or shadow, owner decision) in the upper half, so your head stays in view; the land table waits until play starts.
- **Knockouts (2026-11-01):** a player who goes out bursts into pieces. **Knocked out (cut, or all their land taken; not quitting), the head first spins** (two turns, faster and faster), **pops up and shrinks, then bursts up there** (owner decision, 2026-11-08; `look.outSpin`); a quitter just bursts. The sounds stay on the moment it goes out. Your own knockout of someone stamps `CUT BEN!` (or `TOOK BEN'S LAND!`) in your color over the arena with a ring of stars, and the view gives a light shake; going out yourself gives a hard shake.
- **Out:** a `TabCard` at the bottom: `YOU'RE OUT!` ("Cut by Ana." / "Ana took all your land." / "Your land is gone." / "You quit this match.") for 4 s, then a `WATCHING` badge at the bottom (`WATCHING LIVE` for room members who joined late).
- **Leader's crown (owner decision, 2026-11-08):** during the match, whoever has the most land (as shown) wears a **gold crown faked in 3D like the head** (owner: like Paper.io's; a flat cutout looked terrible, a thin one "made out of paper", one with a lit rim, dark inside and separate prongs "3 different things" and not gold): **one chunky ring in the theme's gold** (`gold` shaded toward `goldLight`), seen at the head's tilt, its band shaded face by face by the light, its thick rim's top in the light, the head's top showing through the hole in its shadow with the inner wall at the back, and five round balls sitting on the rim, turning with the head; one soft darker-gold outline around the ring, none between its parts, standing on the middle of the head's top. It **takes the crest's place** (the feathers poking around it were too busy). It moves to whoever takes the lead, and no one wears it while the top is a tie (as at the start). Their name tag sits above the crown. The ending's big winner heads wear the same crown (`drawCrown.ts`, `look.crown`, `leader.ts`).
- **Ending (owner: "do a good winning animation like tic-tac-toe", 2026-11-01):** the camera pulls out to the whole arena with a thump; everyone but the winners washes out; rays in the winner's color turn behind the arena; a big copy of each winner's head drops onto them wearing the leader's 3D crown, spinning as it drops from whichever way it was heading (one full turn plus the short way round, slowing to a stop) until it faces us, eyes to the camera (owner decision 2026-11-08); then the result tag stamps on near the bottom like tic-tac-toe's (`YOU WIN!` / `SHARED WIN!` in your color with a trophy and your land share counting up to the exact figure, confetti and stars; `YOU LOST` with your place; watchers `ANA WINS!`). No names, minimap, table or "tap" text. It moves on to the results by itself after 6 s; a tap does nothing (owner decision 2026-11-01: a stray tap skipped it). Timings in `look` / `hud` of the client config.
- **Results:** the arena results screen (see DESIGN.md, Scoreboard & results): land share for players still in, time survived for the rest.
- **Tile art (owner decision 2026-11-08: the old small arena in a circle wasn't nice):** a close-up of a match in the game's own look and proportions, filling the tile like tic-tac-toe's board: the dotted floor in an ink frame, red and blue land raised on their lips with studs, red's trail looping out from under its land and heading home with red's head at its end, and blue's head in its land. The heads are drawn by the game's own head code (crest, eyes), loaded with the game's drawing code that the room preloads anyway, so they always match the game; no crown (owner: not needed). A hand-drawn head was turned down: too big, staring eyes, not like the game.

## Sound

Your head is a felt marker on paper, your land is notes: the platform's kit (see DESIGN.md, Sound) with its own character. `sounds.ts` says what each one is for; `territoryCues.ts` picks them; numbers in `sound` of the client config.

- **Trail:** leaving your land is the pen touching down, then one quiet marker loop: one smooth, unbroken sound, because the head moves in one smooth, unbroken motion, so never a beat, ticks or strokes. A little faster and higher the longer you're out; darker, slower and heavier on someone's land (`trailShape`); it stops the moment you're home. The whole build-up is handed to the audio clock once when you leave, and again only when you cross onto or off someone's land (`trailCourse`), so nothing is set each frame.
- **Captures:** three sizes by area (small, medium, large; `captureSize.ts`, also the head's hop): a quick run of rising notes that goes further the bigger the land, then the land settling. Only your own captures sound; what others do to land, yours included, is seen, not heard.
- **Knockouts:** one sound however it happened (cut or land taken), since the screen says which. You knock someone out: a fat pop as the tag lands. You're out: a low blow, then a soft fall. Someone else goes out: a small scatter of pieces, full volume about as far as the screen shows, fading to silent off screen; watchers hear it at one lower volume.
- **Bumps:** rubbery blocks when your head meets another (heads are solid), a dull knock at the wall, once per touch.
- **Ending:** a thump and a long breath of air as the camera pulls out, air whistling down as the winner's head drops, then the platform's result sounds as the tag stamps on. The last seconds of the clock use the platform's timer.

Everything is read from the game loop by `TerritoryAudio`, on what the client knows: own moments when predicted, so they land with what's drawn. Simulation, netcode and drawing know nothing of sound.

## Haptics

`haptics.ts`, on the platform's levels (see DESIGN.md, Haptics); they follow the sounds, so they land with what's drawn. **Android only while playing:** steering is a drag, and iPhones only tick on a tap, so on an iPhone only the HUD's buttons and the screens around the match are felt.

- **Trail:** a `selection` tick as you leave your land, then nothing while you draw, so your hand stays quiet for the moments that matter (no buzz under your thumb).
- **Captures:** three sizes by area, one to three pulses climbing from `light` to `heavy`. Only your own.
- **Knockouts:** you knock someone out: a `heavy` hit and a `medium` echo, with the stamp. You're out: two `heavy` hits, then it falls away through `medium` and `light`, with the hard shake; the hardest thing in a match. Others going out aren't felt, nor are others' captures, your land included.
- **Bumps:** `medium` when your head meets another, `light` at the wall, once per touch.
- **Ending:** a `thump` as the camera pulls out, `medium` as the winner's head drops, then the platform's result as the tag stamps on. The clock's last seconds use the platform's `selection` tick.
