# Sea Battle

Format: **tournament** (Swiss-style, 1v1 matches; see the tournament rules in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md)). Catalog id `seabattle`, 2+ players.

The classic hidden-fleet game: both players place their ships in secret, then take turns firing at the other's waters. A hit fires again. Sink the whole fleet first to win.

> This doc is Sea Battle's source of truth: rules, owner decisions, how it works, its look and its files. Platform-wide rules, the tournament and the room flow are in [`CLAUDE.md`](../../CLAUDE.md); the shared visual language, including the turn-based match screen and its endings, is in [`DESIGN.md`](../../DESIGN.md). Update this doc in the same change whenever something about Sea Battle changes.

---

## Rules (owner decisions, 2026-11-13, unless marked)

- **1v1, a tournament like tic-tac-toe** (owner: a hidden-fleet game is a two-player game).
- **Board: 8×8, a square** (owner decision: not 10×10; a square, because the screen also holds the other board small beside it and the two seat cards, and a square looks the same on phones, sideways phones and laptops).
- **Fleet: 5, 4, 3, 3, 2** (five ships; owner decision: no 1-cell ships at all, and only one 2, since hunting the last small ship is what drags a match out). Almost every ship has its own length, so a sunk list reads clearly.
- **Ships are straight and may touch** (side by side, corner to corner; owner decision 2026-11-13: no gap rule); they never overlap.
- **Placing: both at once, in secret, 60 s.** **You place every ship yourself** (owner decision 2026-11-13: no ready-made layout): the board starts empty with the fleet lying in a **dock** beside it. **Drag** a ship from the dock onto the board (it lands where your finger is, by the part you picked it up by; within half a cell of the board it's kept on it), drag one on the board to move it (only where it fits), or drag it off the board to put it back in the dock. **Tap** a ship to **turn it around the cell you pressed** (owner decision), pushed back onto the board if it would run off. **It always turns, even onto another ship** (owner decision): the ships lying on each other fade, the line says `SHIPS CAN'T OVERLAP!` and READY! waits until it's moved. **SHUFFLE** puts the whole fleet in a random legal layout. **SHUFFLE** and **READY!** sit under the dock; READY! is greyed out until every ship is down and none overlap (once sent, both grey out and the editor stays until the other is ready). When the time runs out, your fleet is sent for you: the ships you put down stay, and any still in the dock (or lying on another) go in random spots where they fit. A phone that isn't there gets a random fleet from the server (from a fresh random number, so no phone can know it ahead).
- **Who fires first** is the seat that starts (the platform's Fair starts rule: fewer starts in this tournament, else random).
- **Firing: press and hold on their waters to aim, let go to fire** (owner decision 2026-11-13): a crosshair follows your finger; slide off the board to cancel; letting go on a cell already shot does nothing. A focused cell fires with Enter or Space. **A hit fires again, a miss passes the turn** (owner decision). **30 s per shot** (the same as the other turn-based games); the countdown shows only in the last 5 s. When it runs out, the **first untouched cell of the other's waters in reading order** is fired at (no luck).
- **A single hit tells you nothing more** (owner decision: the rest of the ship could be above, below, left or right; guessing is the fun).
- **A sunk ship shows to both players**, burnt. Nothing round it is marked: ships may touch, so the next cell could hold another.
- **Your own ships don't show either** once firing starts (owner decision 2026-11-13: you placed them, you know where they are; nobody needs to keep seeing their placement): both boards look the same on both phones, only shots, burnt hits and sunk ships, until the end.
- **First to sink the whole fleet wins, at once** (owner decision 2026-11-13, replacing a last turn for the player who fired second: it confused players, and it only changed about 1 match in 20, where both needed exactly as many turns; who starts is shared fairly anyway, CLAUDE.md, Fair starts). No draws.
- **Watchers** (a bye, a quitter, a late joiner) **see only the shots** (owner decision): hits, misses and sunk ships on both boards, no ship until it sinks, so nobody next to a player can give a fleet away. Both fleets show to everyone once the match is over.
- Quitting, timeouts claimed by the phones and points are the tournament's (CLAUDE.md).

## Numbers (`src/shared/games/seabattle/config.ts`)

`boardSize` 8, `fleet` [5, 4, 3, 3, 2] (a ship's place in this list is its index everywhere), `placeTimeMs` 60 000, `turnTimeMs` 30 000. Cell `row × 8 + col`; that number is a shot on the wire. Client-only (`src/web/games/seabattle/config.ts`): the fleet on screen is sent `autoReadyLeadMs` (800 ms) before placing runs out, a press that moves less than `dragThresholdPx` (8) is a tap, and your cannonball that lands with no answer from the server never counted after `shotAnswerWaitMs` (2.5 s). Placing sizes (`src/theme/spacing.ts`): the board fits the screen's height less `seaReservePlacing` (portrait: the dock and buttons under it) or `seaReserveWide` (landscape); the column beside it `seaSidePlacing` (320px) on a laptop or tablet, `seaSide` (200px) on a sideways phone. Firing, the stage sizes itself from the room the screen leaves it (`stageGeometry.ts`, in big-board widths): the small board 0.34 of the big one (owner: smaller), the strip 0.1, gaps 0.04, the cannon 0.22 across. Tiles (`seaTiles`, a share of their board's width): water 1.3%, corners 2.2%, lip 0.9%. Timings (`src/theme/motion.ts`): the other's cannon swings onto the cell in `cannonTurn` (240 ms), a ball flies `shotFlight` (560 ms), its splash or blast has `shotSettle` (650 ms) before the boards trade places in `boardSwap` (520 ms), then the next cannon comes in; a puff of smoke drifts off in `smoke` (3.6 s).

How long a match lasts: a strong computer player needs roughly 30 shots to sink this fleet on 8×8 (measured with the old no-touching rule; touching ships give no free water, so a little more); people need more.

## How it works

- **State** (`SeaBattleState`): the phase (`place` / `fire`), who starts and whose shot it is, each seat's fleet (`null` until ready) and `ready`, each seat's waters as shot at (`marks`: none, miss, hit), the ships sunk (`wrecks`, with where they lay), the shot count, the last shot, the winner. It travels in the room view as JSON, like tic-tac-toe's.
- **Secrets on the wire.** The server keeps every fleet; **each phone gets its own room view** (`tournamentView` → the game's `viewFor`): your own fleet only, watchers none, until the match is over. `RoomService` sends one view per player (`viewsDiffer`) instead of one broadcast while such a game is played. Wrecks carry their own position, so a sunk ship can be drawn without the fleet.
- **The platform's turn-based match, extended** (optional parts of `MatchGame`, used only by Sea Battle so far): `turnTimeMs(state)` (placing gets 60 s), `keepsClock` (one player getting ready doesn't restart the other's placing time; firing starts a fresh clock), `expire(state, seed)` (a timeout while placing gives everyone not ready a random fleet from the server's fresh random number; while firing, the auto shot) and `viewFor`. On the client the match look says who must act (`toMove`: both while placing, so both chips are lit and each phone takes its own player's wash), that the players show as slim chips with avatars (`cards: "slim"`, `pieces: false`), and gets your seat, the names and when the turn ends.
- **Rules** are pure (`rules.ts`, `fleet.ts`); the server validates every fleet (whole, legal), shot, turn and seat.
- **Client:** the shared match screen (`MATCH_LOOKS`, layout `twoBoards`) with Sea Battle's board in it (`SeaBattleBoard`): the fleet editor while placing, then the firing stage.
- **Shots play out before their result shows** (`useVolley`): a new shot in the room view starts a volley while rendering (so a result never shows for a frame early), and the game from before the shot is drawn until its ball lands; after a miss the boards trade places once its splash has had its moment. Your own ball leaves as you let go, before the server answers (no wait on the network); the result fills in when the answer comes, normally while it's still in the air. The board tells the match screen whose turn it is showing (`onShownToMove`), so the lit chip and the wash change with the boards, never ahead of them. The shot that ends the match plays out too, on every phone: its look says so (`playsMovesOut`), so the match screen holds its ending until the board says that ball is down (`onLastMoveShown`, at most `lastMoveWaitMs`), and the ending starts as it lands, playing its landing. Several shots at once (back from sleep) just show. Everything that moves is CSS animating only `translate`, `scale`, `rotate` and `opacity` (the compositor's, smooth on any phone): no per-frame code, a handful of renders per shot.

## Cost (free tier)

Like tic-tac-toe per move: each fleet and each shot is one WebSocket message and one SQLite row write, nothing between turns. A match is 2 fleets plus about 60–90 shots. While a match is played every change sends one room view per player instead of one broadcast (the same number of messages out, a little more CPU to build them). The room view is about 1 KB larger than a tic-tac-toe one. Not measured yet.

## Look

The platform's look and colors, kept as they are (owner decision 2026-11-13: improve the layout and the ships, not the theme). My calls unless marked:

- **Nothing scrolls, on any screen** (owner decision): the players are **slim chips** in one row beside the corner ✕ (avatar, name, the last seconds; lit while they must act, then the winner; on small phones the name takes the avatar's room), then a short status line, then the **stage**, sized from the room left (measured, so it fits whatever is around it: chips, a watcher's header and footer).
- **Room to breathe** (owner decision): the chips sit on top beside the ✕; the stage is centered in the height left, never packed at the top.
- **The stage** (owner decision 2026-11-13: zoom the board being fired at, the other small, with the shooter's cannon on it): both waters at once, **theirs on top and yours under** (in landscape yours on the left, theirs on the right, matching the chips). The one being fired at is big, the other drawn at 0.34 with the shooter's **cannon** on it; **between them the strip**: their fleet in a row by their waters, yours by yours (in landscape it stands up, its rows columns), each ship small in its owner's color, burnt once sunk. The boards **trade sizes, never places**: after a miss, once the splash has had its moment, the big one shrinks and the small one grows while the strip glides with them (only `translate` and `scale`), then the other's cannon pops in on the board that just went small. Theirs is big again at the end (every ship they had shows). Watchers see it from the left player's side.
- **The cannon** (owner: the first ones looked bad, too thin and too tall; this one follows the owner's close-up reference): chunky, in **bold outlines that scale with it** (so a small one keeps its look), seen a little from behind: a round mount in the shooter's color, shaded on its far side, casting a soft shadow, and a **short, fat iron barrel** about half the mount's width (`iron`, a neutral grey), rounded at the back, a shine down its lit side, its **muzzle a thick rim** facing you with the inside wall (`ironDark`) showing above the black bore. It points at the middle of the big board, never swinging the long way round. **Yours follows your finger** while you hold to aim. **Theirs** swings onto the cell when their shot comes in.
- **Firing** (owner: make it good): the whole cannon squashes and swells, its barrel kicks back, a white-hot flash blooms at the muzzle and a puff of smoke drifts off it. The **ball** (round iron lit from the top left, ink outline) is thrown: its **shadow** slides straight over the water to the cell while the ball rises above it on screen and grows to the top of its arc (fast up, slowing, then falling faster: `rise` and `fall` easings), then drops onto it; only when it lands does the result show.
- **Landing:** a **miss** splashes (a white ring spreading, drops thrown up), its tile sinking; a **hit** blasts (a white-hot flash through gold, embers, a puff of smoke; the board stays still, owner: no shaking); a **sinking** blasts bigger and the burnt ship slams down onto its cells.
- **Background:** the match screen's wash, as in every game: whoever must act (as the stage shows it: the shooter's until their ball has landed and the boards have traded places); while both place, each phone in its own player's color (owner: no dotted page). A draw keeps the platform's plain page.
- **Status line** (the platform's voice; the chips stay word-free): `PLACE YOUR SHIPS!`, `WAITING FOR BEN`, your first shot `HOLD TO AIM, LET GO TO FIRE!`, then `YOUR TURN: FIRE!`, `HIT! FIRE AGAIN!`, `SUNK! FIRE AGAIN!`; theirs `BEN IS FIRING AT YOU`, `BEN HIT! FIRING AGAIN`, `BEN SANK YOUR SHIP!`. Watchers: `PLACING SHIPS`, then `BEN IS FIRING`.
- **Waters** (owner: the cells must feel like they're in the water): the board is **water** (`sea`, light blue) with 64 pale rounded **tiles floating on it**, lit from above (`seaTileLight` to `seaTile`, a soft lip `seaLip` along the bottom), the water showing round and between them, in the shared ink-outlined board frame (`BoardFrame`, `sea` spacing). Their sizes are shares of the board's own width (`cqw`), so the small board is the same drawing, smaller.
- **Ships** (owner: real ships, not rounded bars, each with its own outline, and **each one single outline with every part inside it**, nothing sticking out, so a whole ship can be filled one color at once): drawn from above in the owner's color with ink outlines, so they tell apart at a glance: the 5 an **aircraft carrier** (angular flight deck, the angled landing strip jutting out to one side, catapults along the bow, the island on a bulge of the other edge), the 4 a **battleship** (long and widest amidships with a sharp bow and a round stern, three turrets, a stepped bridge and its funnel), the first 3 a **cruiser** (slim, a square stern, two turrets, two funnels), the second 3 a **submarine** (a rounded cigar with its tail fins, the sail with its planes across), the 2 a **patrol boat** (short and beamy, a square stern with two outboard motors on it, a cabin with its windshield, a small gun). Bow to the right lying across, down standing. Once firing starts **no afloat ship shows** (owner decision), yours included; a **sunk one, anyone's, is burnt** (owner: a blasted look, not its color; `char` hull, `charDeck` deck, `charPart` fittings, still in one outline) and slams down as it goes, its tiles open under it; at the end the ones never found show paler in their color. While placing, ships have **no button lip** (owner decision: they look like ships, not buttons); a ship being dragged grows a little, and fades where it doesn't fit, with its old place pale underneath; carried off the board it follows the finger at the board's size. Ships lying on each other (after a turn) fade. The dock (owner: roomy, not packed) lies the ships across with wide gaps, each keeping its place as the others leave (and the dock its room once empty, so nothing moves), a cell as big as fits up to `space-8`: under the board in portrait across its whole width, in two rows (the 5 and the 4; the 3s and the 2), above SHUFFLE and READY! side by side; beside the board on a laptop or tablet in a wider column (`seaSidePlacing`), the same two rows above the two buttons stacked; on a sideways phone in the usual narrow column, three rows (the 5; the 4 and the 2; the 3s), starting level with the status line.
- **Aiming:** while you hold, a ring sits on the cell under your finger (your light color, ink outline, a dot in the middle) with thin lines in your color along its row and column; faded on a cell already shot.
- **Marks** (owner: the old dots and crosses didn't suit the platform; flames were tried and dropped, owner: worse than none): a **miss** sinks its tile, leaving open water with a faint white **ripple**; a **hit** on a ship not yet sunk is a **burnt tile** (`char`, lit from above like the others; owner: a dark square that gives nothing away about which way the ship lies) with a little **smoke** rising off it; a **sunk ship** lies burnt in the water, a fuller wisp off its middle. Smoke is soft puffs with no edge (`smoke`, fading out round it), three to a wisp, drifting up and a little downwind as they swell and fade (owner: subtle, only to look good). The newest mark comes in as its ball lands. Smoke is hidden for anyone who asks their phone for less motion.
- **Endings** are the platform's (DESIGN.md, Match screen), on the big board (theirs): it thumps, the tiles fade so the winner's color comes through under the ships, the result tag sits on its bottom edge, over the strip, the winner gets sun rays, stars and confetti (no win line, so no sparks).
- **Tile art** (owner: the first, the whole board small with a burnt wreck, was too plain; ships afloat on it misled, since the game never shows them; an aiming crosshair was too much): a shot on its way, close up: a corner of the waters being fired at, about four tiles across with the rest running off the edges, only a few things a player sees there: a cruiser already sunk, burnt black, along the bottom; two burnt tiles in the second row where another ship is being found; two misses on the water; and a cannonball (`IronBall`, the game's own, drawn bigger so it reads) coming down at an angle onto the tile just left of those hits (as a player would aim), its small faint shadow on that tile showing where it lands and a white dashed arc over the top into it, the path it came along.

## Sound

**Real-sounding, not the toy box** (owner decision 2026-11-13: the first set, built on the platform's pops, knocks and little note jingles, sounded fake; DESIGN.md, Sound, notes the exception): a real cannon and water, all made by our own code (`seaKit.ts`), at the platform's levels. No notes or jingles: who it happened to is in how near and heavy it is (your own ship hit is nearer and heavier than your hit; watchers hear it from further off). **No sinking sound** (owner decision 2026-11-13: none fitted; versions built from the splash and the hit, rolling explosions (thunder), a ship's bell and a bugle were all turned down): a sinking plays the hit, and the big blast and the wreck slamming down on screen carry the moment, with its own feel. **Every sound is one sound** (owner: layers that come apart, like an echo or drops after a splash, read as several; and one take each, owner: takes a hair apart couldn't be told apart). How they're built: **a miss** (`bubbleSplash`) is thousands of tiny bubbles, each a short ring a few cycles long climbing a little in pitch as it closes, spread over 18 bands, loudest in the upper middle: a bright slap as it breaks the surface, a breath, then the body swelling in, surging and lulling, dying away within a second. What makes it a splash is that it's **choppy**: every band jumps up and down a few dB every few milliseconds on its own (the lows more), neighbours partly together (owner: smooth, it's a wave on the shore; one ringing note sounded like strings, pianos and drums; shaped noise like folding paper). **A gun or a hit** is a burst of smooth noise darkening fast over a low punch, a hit brighter and harder with the ship splintering in the same instant. A ship on the board is one hollow wooden thock; a ship moving is air, smooth dark noise whose brightness opens and closes (owner: plain noise whooshes sounded like sandpaper); the cannon's metal is a click or a clunk. Sounds follow the **stage**, not the network (`useSeaBattleSounds`): a shot is heard as its ball leaves and lands on screen, never when the room view comes in, so the match screen's usual move sounds are off (`moveSteps` empty) and it plays only the last shot's landing as the ending starts (the watchers' version: the ending's own sounds say who won). Someone else's shot sits back a little, watching a little more; your ships' news is yours, at full volume. The other's cannon swings onto its cell silently (owner decision: no sound needed).

## Haptics

Felt (DESIGN.md, Haptics: your hand, your news): placing, a ship picked up (`selection`), put down (`light`), turned or docked (`selection`), SHUFFLE as a button (`light`); your cannon firing as you let go (`medium`, from the board, since everyone hears the boom); your hit (`medium`), your sinking (`medium`, then `heavy` as the wreck slams); your ship hit (`heavy`), your ship sunk (`heavy`, `heavy` as the wreck slams, a late `light`); your turn (`light`, as your cannon clunks in after the boards trade places, from the board). Most come with their sound (`soundHaptics.ts`); firing, your turn and a sinking (which plays the hit's sound, its feel taking over from the hit's) are played by the board (`useSeaBattleSounds`). iPhones feel only taps on buttons (SHUFFLE, READY!): Apple's tick needs a hidden switch under a real tap, and ships are dragged (a turn is a tap on a ship you also drag) and firing is a hold, so neither carries one. Not felt: the other's cannon and their splashes, aiming (nothing buzzes while you steer), anything while watching. The ending is the platform's.
